\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{HeteroInherNet-IB}, a principled extension of the InherNet
framework~\cite{zhou2026inherNet} that replaces uniform low-rank inheritance
with \emph{data-aware heterogeneous} rank allocation.
Building on the original one-down-many-ups asymmetric Mixture-of-Experts
(MoE) architecture and SVD-based knowledge inheritance,
HeteroInherNet-IB introduces four mutually reinforcing improvements:
(i)~activation-whitened spectral analysis for data-aligned decomposition,
(ii)~von~Neumann entropy-driven per-layer rank budgeting with temperature
smoothing,
(iii)~decoupled gating with a routing-floor policy, and
(iv)~de-symmetrized expert initialization with load-balance regularization.
Collectively, these modifications preserve InherNet's convergence guarantees
at the level of parameterization and admit analogous stationarity analyses
under explicit boundedness assumptions. They also provide a data-weighted
low-rank approximation principle and an information-bottleneck interpretation,
rather than claiming an unconditional information-theoretic optimum for deep
nonlinear networks.
\end{abstract}

% ============================================================================
\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}

Neural network inheritance (NNI) aims to compress a pretrained teacher
network by performing low-rank decomposition on its weight matrices and
constructing a lightweight yet expressive inheritor. InherNet~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}
formalizes this idea through two mechanisms: (1)~\emph{knowledge inheritance}
via truncated SVD, and (2)~\emph{structure inheritance} via an asymmetric
one-down-many-ups MoE module.  Both linear and convolutional layers
are decomposed, and the resulting factorized modules are trained end-to-end,
optionally with knowledge distillation.

A key limitation of the original InherNet is its \emph{uniform rank policy}:
every layer receives the same rank~$r$, irrespective of its spectral
complexity or its role in the network.  This policy can over-compress
information-rich layers while wasting budget on layers whose spectra
decay rapidly.  In this work we propose
\textbf{HeteroInherNet-IB} (\textbf{Hetero}geneous \textbf{Inher}itance
\textbf{Net}work guided by the \textbf{I}nformation \textbf{B}ottleneck),
which closes this gap by introducing a data-driven, entropy-budgeted,
per-layer rank allocation scheme together with several complementary
training-stability improvements.

\section{Preliminaries: Original InherNet}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Notation.}
Let $\bW\!\in\!\R^{m\times n}$ denote a pretrained teacher weight matrix
(or the reshaped convolution kernel
$\hat{\bm{K}}\!\in\!\R^{N\times(c k_w k_h)}$).
Its thin SVD is $\bW=\bU\bSigma\bV^\top$, and the rank-$r$ truncation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:svd-trunc}
  \bW_r = \bU_r\,\bSigma_r\,\bV_r^\top,
  \qquad
  \|\bW-\bW_r\|_F^2 = \sum_{i=r+1}^{\min(m,n)}\sigma_i^2(\bW),
\end{equation}
by the Eckart--Young--Mirsky theorem~\cite{eckart1936approximation}.

\paragraph{Knowledge inheritance.}
InherNet approximates the \emph{full} pretrained weight $\bW$
(not the LoRA-style update $\Delta\bW$), factorizing it into two
consecutive low-rank layers initialized by truncated SVD.

\paragraph{Structure inheritance.}
Given input features $\bX$, the InherNet module output is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inherNet}
  \bY = \sum_{h=1}^{H} G_h(\bX)\;\bW_h^{\up}\!\bigl(\bW^{\down}(\bX)\bigr),
  \qquad
  G(\bX) = \softmax(\bW_g(\bX)),
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the number of expert heads. The shared backbone
$\bW^{\down}$ reduces dimensionality and each expert head
$\bW_h^{\up}$ reconstructs the output, producing a one-down-many-ups
topology.  Under the column-vector convention $y=\bW x$,
initialization follows a balanced $\bSigma^{1/2}$ split:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:init-orig}
  \bW^{\down} \leftarrow \bSigma_r^{1/2}\bV_r^\top,
  \qquad
  \bW_h^{\up} \leftarrow \bU_r\,\bSigma_r^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Equivalent transposed forms appear when features are written as row vectors.
The important point is that the two factors reconstruct
$\bU_r\bSigma_r\bV_r^\top$ before task training.

\paragraph{Core theoretical properties (from~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Convergence}: Under standard non-convex SGD assumptions
  (Lipschitz gradient, bounded variance, bounded representations),
  InherNet achieves
  $\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\E[\|\nabla\cL(\theta^{(t)})\|^2]=\cO(1/T)$,
  with a practical scaling constant improved from $L$ to
  $L'\approx L/\kappa$ due to orthonormal SVD initialization
  (Proposition~2.3, Theorem~2.4 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}).
\item \textbf{Information bottleneck}: The one-down-many-ups design is
  analyzed as creating
  a single shared bottleneck $\bZ\triangleq\bW^{\down}(\bX)$ that forms
  the Markov chain $\bX\to\bZ\to\bY$ and motivates the IB Lagrangian
  $\cL_{\mathrm{IB}}=I(\bX;\bZ)-\beta\,I(\bZ;\bY)$
  (Appendix~A.2 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}).
\item \textbf{Knowledge preservation}: Rank is the primary determinant
  of inherited knowledge; the functional similarity between teacher
  $f_{\bW}$ and inheritor $f_r$ satisfies
  $\mathrm{Sim}(f_{\bW},f_r)
    \ge 1-\sum_l\alpha_l(1-\sum_{i=1}^r\sigma_{l,i}^2/\sum_j\sigma_{l,j}^2)$,
  which is monotonically increasing in $r$ but \emph{not} in $H$
  (Proposition~2.7 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}).
\item \textbf{Diminishing returns of heads}: The marginal error reduction
  from adding the $(H\!+\!1)$-th head satisfies
  $\mathcal{E}(r,H)-\mathcal{E}(r,H\!+\!1)\le\frac{c}{H^2}\mathcal{E}(r,1)$
  (Proposition~2.10 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}).
\end{enumerate}
These results establish that rank allocation is the \emph{primary lever} for
controlling the quality of inheritance. HeteroInherNet-IB directly builds
on this insight.

\section{Proposed Method: HeteroInherNet-IB}
\label{sec:method}

We retain the asymmetric one-down-many-ups backbone of InherNet but
replace the uniform rank assignment with a data-aware heterogeneous
allocation pipeline.  The six components of HeteroInherNet-IB are
described below and summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:hetero}.

% ---------- 3.1 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Inheritance Source: Trained-Source Initialization}
\label{sec:copy}

A prerequisite for meaningful SVD-based inheritance is that the weight
matrices being decomposed already encode learned representations.
In the original InherNet, the teacher's pretrained weights are directly
decomposed; in the hetero setting, we additionally require a calibration
forward pass (Section~\ref{sec:whitening}).  We therefore describe the
source model abstractly as
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{\mathrm{source}}
  \in
  \{\theta_{\mathrm{teacher}},\;
    \theta_{\mathrm{trained\ compact}},\;
    \theta_{\mathrm{copied\ base}}\}.
\end{equation}
The concrete choice can depend on the experimental protocol.  For
paper-style CIFAR-100 inheritance, the natural source is the trained
teacher; for the legacy CIFAR-10 workflow that reproduces
\texttt{demo\_code\_org.py}, the source can be a trained compact
baseline copied into the heterogeneous base.

The paper-level principle is thus not ``copy only'', but rather
\emph{trained-source inheritance}: heterogeneous decomposition should
not be applied to random weights.  This ensures that the singular
spectrum being truncated reflects task-relevant structure.

% ---------- 3.2 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Activation Whitening Before Decomposition}
\label{sec:whitening}

Standard SVD of $\bW$ treats all input directions equally.  When the
input activation covariance is non-isotropic, however, the Frobenius-norm
approximation error $\|\bW-\bW_r\|_F$ may poorly reflect the
\emph{functional} error observed in practice.

\paragraph{Covariance estimation.}
Given a calibration set $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, we estimate the channel-wise
input covariance at each convolutional layer $l$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov}
  \bSigma_x^{(l)}
    = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bar{x}_i^{(l)}\,\bar{x}_i^{(l)\top}
    + \epsilon\,\bI,
\end{equation}
where $\bar{x}^{(l)}=\E_{h,w}[x^{(l)}_{:,h,w}]$ denotes the
spatially-averaged activation and $\epsilon>0$ is a regularization
constant.
For a linear layer, $\bar{x}^{(l)}$ is the full input feature vector.
For convolutional layers this channel covariance is a tractable
approximation to a full im2col covariance.

\paragraph{Cholesky whitening.}
We compute the Cholesky factor $\bC^{(l)}$ such that
$\bSigma_x^{(l)}=\bC^{(l)}\bC^{(l)\top}$, and form the whitened weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whiten}
  \widetilde{\bW}^{(l)} = \bW^{(l)}\,\bC^{(l)}.
\end{equation}
All subsequent spectral analysis is performed on $\widetilde{\bW}^{(l)}$,
whose singular values now reflect the importance of weight directions
\emph{as weighted by the data distribution}.

\begin{remark}[Relation to the IB principle]
Whitening the weight by $\bC$ is equivalent to changing the metric
under which the rank-$r$ approximation is evaluated from the Frobenius
norm to a \emph{data-weighted} norm
$\|\bW-\bW_r\|_{\bSigma_x}^2=\tr[(\bW-\bW_r)\bSigma_x(\bW-\bW_r)^\top]$.
This aligns the compression objective with the mutual-information
term $I(\bX;\bZ)$ in the IB Lagrangian: directions of $\bW$ that
carry little variance under $p(\bX)$ contribute negligibly to
$I(\bX;\bZ)$ and are safely discarded.
\end{remark}

% ---------- 3.3 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Spectral-Entropy-Driven Heterogeneous Rank Allocation}
\label{sec:rank}

Having obtained the whitened SVD
$\widetilde{\bW}^{(l)}=\widetilde{\bU}^{(l)}\widetilde{\bSigma}^{(l)}
\widetilde{\bV}^{(l)\top}$, we quantify the \emph{spectral complexity}
of each layer via von~Neumann entropy on the squared spectrum.

\paragraph{Layer-wise spectral entropy.}
Define the normalized squared-singular-value distribution:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-p}
  p_{l,i} = \frac{\sigma_{l,i}^{2}}{\sum_j \sigma_{l,j}^{2}},
\end{equation}
and the spectral entropy:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
  \cH_l = -\sum_{i} p_{l,i}\,\log p_{l,i}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Why $\sigma^2$ instead of $\sigma$]
The choice of squaring the singular values before normalization is not
a mere convention.  It corresponds to using the \emph{normalized
Gram-spectrum distribution}
$\lambda_i=\sigma_i^2/\|\bW\|_F^2$ of the Gram matrix $\bW^\top\bW$.
This is the standard von~Neumann entropy
$S(\rho)=-\tr(\rho\log\rho)$ of the quantum-information-theoretic
density operator $\rho=\bW^\top\bW/\tr(\bW^\top\bW)$, which has a direct
interpretation: it equals the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalue spectrum
and quantifies the \emph{effective rank}~\cite{roy2007effective}
of the operator.
Using $\sigma$ directly (without squaring) would instead measure
entropy over the singular-value distribution, which lacks this
operator-theoretic grounding and under-weights the tails of the spectrum.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Temperature-smoothed budget allocation.}
Given a global budget $\cB$ (expressed as a fraction of total maximum
ranks), a minimum rank floor $r_{\min}$, and a temperature parameter
$\tau\ge 1$, we allocate ranks as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-alloc}
  r_l = r_{\min}
    + \left\lfloor
      \frac{\cH_l^{1/\tau}}
           {\sum_{j}\cH_j^{1/\tau}}
      \cdot
      \bigl(\cB - L\,r_{\min}\bigr)
    \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the number of decomposed layers and
$\lfloor\cdot\rceil$ denotes rounding to the nearest integer,
followed by a greedy correction pass that adjusts ranks to satisfy
$\sum_l r_l = \cB$ exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy-weighted budget allocation]
\label{prop:alloc}
Let $a_l=\cH_l^{1/\tau}$ and let $s_l=r_l-r_{\min}$ denote the
extra rank assigned above the floor.  The continuous relaxation
\begin{equation}
  \max_{s_l\ge 0}
  \sum_l a_l\log(s_l+\epsilon)
  \quad
  \text{s.t.}\quad
  \sum_l s_l=\cB-Lr_{\min}
\end{equation}
allocates the extra budget proportionally to $a_l$ up to the small
$\epsilon$ correction.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:rank-alloc} is the corresponding
integer-rounded allocation with clipping by layer-wise maximum rank.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Lagrangian is
$\sum_l a_l\log(s_l+\epsilon)-\lambda(\sum_l s_l-\cB+Lr_{\min})$.
Stationarity gives $a_l/(s_l+\epsilon)=\lambda$, hence
$s_l=a_l/\lambda-\epsilon$.  Enforcing the budget constraint yields
$s_l$ proportional to $a_l$ when $\epsilon$ is small relative to the
available extra budget.  The implementation then rounds, clips, and
greedily corrects the allocation to satisfy the discrete budget.
\end{proof}

The temperature $\tau>1$ acts as a smoothing parameter that
interpolates between entropy-proportional allocation ($\tau=1$) and a
near-uniform assignment ($\tau\to\infty$).
In practice $\tau=1.4$ provides a good trade-off between fidelity
and robustness by preventing extreme rank assignments in layers with
atypically high or low entropy.

The minimum rank floor $r_{\min}$ (default 8) prevents rank collapse:
with $r_l<r_{\min}$, the compressed representation may fall below
the minimum dimensionality required for the gating network to
meaningfully specialize experts, and the resulting gradient signal
becomes degenerate.

% ---------- 3.4 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Decoupled Gating with Routing Floor}
\label{sec:gating}

In the original InherNet, the gating network
$G(\bX)=\softmax(\bW_g\,\bar{\bX})$ operates on the \emph{compressed}
representation (the output of $\bW^{\down}$).
Under heterogeneous allocation, however, some layers may receive very
small ranks, leaving the compressed representation too low-dimensional
for effective routing.

We introduce a \emph{routing floor} $r_{\mathrm{route}}$: when
$r_l<r_{\mathrm{route}}$, the gating input is the uncompressed
activation $\bar{\bX}$ (spatially pooled); otherwise, it defaults to
the compressed features.  Formally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate-decouple}
  \text{gate input} =
  \begin{cases}
    \E_{h,w}[\bX]\in\R^{c_{\mathrm{in}}}
      & \text{if } r_l < r_{\mathrm{route}}, \\[2pt]
    \E_{h,w}[\bW^{\down}(\bX)]\in\R^{r_l}
      & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

This decoupled policy ensures that the gating network always receives a
sufficiently informative feature vector, preventing the routing from
degenerating into uniform selection in aggressively compressed layers.
The resulting module is a \texttt{DecoupledGatedSVDConv2d} that
maintains a cached gating probability $\bar{G}$ for use in the
load-balance loss (Section~\ref{sec:balance}).

% ---------- 3.5 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{De-Symmetrized Expert Initialization with Noise Injection}
\label{sec:desym}

A known failure mode in MoE training is \emph{expert collapse}: when
all experts are initialized identically, the gating gradients experience
a saddle point and experts fail to specialize.  The original InherNet
initializes expert heads from the same SVD factors, relying on stochastic
training dynamics to break symmetry.

In HeteroInherNet-IB, after restoring to the original (un-whitened) space
via $\bC^{-1}$, the initialization becomes:
\begin{align}
  \bW^{\down} &\;\leftarrow\;
    \widetilde{\bSigma}_r^{1/2}\,\widetilde{\bV}_r^\top\,(\bC^{(l)})^{-1},
    \label{eq:init-down}\\[3pt]
  \bW_h^{\up} &\;\leftarrow\;
    \widetilde{\bU}_r\,\widetilde{\bSigma}_r^{1/2}
    + \epsilon_h,
    \qquad
    \sum_{h=1}^{H}\epsilon_h=\bm{0},
    \label{eq:init-up}
\end{align}
where $\epsilon_h$ is a small zero-mean perturbation and
$\alpha=0.01$ controls its scale.  The zero-mean constraint preserves
the average SVD reconstruction while making expert outputs distinct.

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry-breaking gradient]
\label{prop:break}
Under the noise-perturbed initialization~\eqref{eq:init-up} with
$\epsilon_h\ne\epsilon_{h'}$ for $h\ne h'$, the gradient of the task
loss with respect to the gating parameters $\theta_g$ is generically
non-zero in directions that differentiate experts:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\theta_g}\cL
  = \sum_{h=1}^{H}\Delta_h\,\nabla_{\theta_g}G_h(\bX)
  \;\neq\; \bm{0},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_h=\partial\cL/\partial f_h(\bX)$, except on a
measure-zero set where the perturbations produce identical expert
responses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since each $\bW_h^{\up}$ differs by a small perturbation $\epsilon_h$,
the expert outputs
$f_h(\bX)=\bW_h^{\up}(\bW^{\down}(\bX))$ are no longer identical.
By Lemma~B.1 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet} (gradient decomposition),
the gating gradient is
$\nabla_{\theta_g}\cL=\sum_h\Delta_h\nabla_{\theta_g}G_h$.
When experts are identical, $\Delta_1=\cdots=\Delta_H$ and
$\sum_h\nabla_{\theta_g}G_h=\nabla_{\theta_g}\sum_h G_h=\bm{0}$
(since softmax sums to 1).  The perturbation breaks this degeneracy:
$\Delta_h\ne\Delta_{h'}$ for almost all nonzero perturbations and
non-degenerate inputs, so the weighted sum is generically nonzero.
\end{proof}

% ---------- 3.6 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Load-Balance Auxiliary Loss}
\label{sec:balance}

Even after symmetry-breaking, expert utilization can become imbalanced
during training.  Following the Switch Transformer
paradigm~\cite{fedus2022switch}, we add a lightweight load-balance
regularizer.  For each \texttt{DecoupledGatedSVDConv2d} module, let
$\bar{G}_h=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^B G_h(x_i)$ be the mean gating
probability over a mini-batch.  The per-module balance loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balance}
  \cL_{\mathrm{bal}}
  = H\cdot\sum_{h=1}^{H}\bar{G}_h^{\,2}.
\end{equation}
The $H$-scaling ensures that the penalty is invariant to the number
of experts. The total training objective aggregates over all
decomposed modules:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total-loss}
  \cL = \cL_{\mathrm{task}}
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}\;
      \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}
      \sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\cL_{\mathrm{bal}}^{(m)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of decomposed layers and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}=0.01$ by default.  Optionally, when
knowledge distillation is employed, $\cL_{\mathrm{task}}$ is replaced by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kd}
  \cL_{\mathrm{task}}
  = \alpha\,\cL_{\mathrm{CE}}(f_r(\bX),y)
  + (1-\alpha)\,T_{\mathrm{KD}}^2\,
    \mathrm{KL}\!\bigl(
      \softmax(f_{\bW}(\bX)/T_{\mathrm{KD}})
      \;\big\|\;
      \softmax(f_r(\bX)/T_{\mathrm{KD}})
    \bigr).
\end{equation}

% ---------- 3.7 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Spectral Cache Reuse}
\label{sec:cache}

The activation whitening (Section~\ref{sec:whitening}) and spectral
entropy computation (Section~\ref{sec:rank}) both require
the Cholesky factor $\bC^{(l)}$ and the SVD triplet
$(\widetilde{\bU}^{(l)},\widetilde{\bSigma}^{(l)},\widetilde{\bV}^{(l)})$.
Rather than recomputing these during the decomposition phase
(Section~\ref{sec:desym}), we cache all spectral factors from the
entropy pass and reuse them, reducing the overall offline cost from
$2\times$ to $1\times$ SVD per layer.  This is purely an engineering
optimization and does not affect the resulting model.

\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{HeteroInherNet-IB: Heterogeneous Neural Network Inheritance}
\label{alg:hetero}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Trained source model with weights $\{\bW^{(l)}\}_{l=1}^L$;
  calibration loader $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{cal}}$;
  budget ratio $\beta$; min rank $r_{\min}$;
  temperature $\tau$; routing floor $r_{\mathrm{route}}$;
  number of expert heads $H$; noise scale $\alpha$;
  balance weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}$.
\ENSURE Decomposed HeteroInherNet-IB model.
\medskip
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 1: Spectral Analysis}
\FOR{each convolutional layer $l=1,\dots,L$}
  \STATE Estimate input covariance
    $\bSigma_x^{(l)}\leftarrow\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\bar{x}_i^{(l)}
    \bar{x}_i^{(l)\top}+\epsilon\bI$ \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov}}
  \STATE Cholesky: $\bC^{(l)}\leftarrow\mathrm{chol}(\bSigma_x^{(l)})$
  \STATE Whiten: $\widetilde{\bW}^{(l)}\leftarrow\bW^{(l)}\bC^{(l)}$
    \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:whiten}}
  \STATE SVD:
    $\widetilde{\bU}^{(l)},\widetilde{\bSigma}^{(l)},\widetilde{\bV}^{(l)}
    \leftarrow\mathrm{svd}(\widetilde{\bW}^{(l)})$
  \STATE Compute entropy
    $\cH_l=-\sum_i p_{l,i}\log p_{l,i}$ with $p_{l,i}=\sigma_{l,i}^2/\sum_j\sigma_{l,j}^2$
    \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropy}}
  \STATE Cache $\bigl\{\bC^{(l)},\widetilde{\bU}^{(l)},
    \widetilde{\bSigma}^{(l)},\widetilde{\bV}^{(l)}\bigr\}$
\ENDFOR
\medskip
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 2: Heterogeneous Rank Allocation}
\STATE Compute global budget: $\cB\leftarrow\max(L\cdot r_{\min},\;\lfloor\beta\cdot\sum_l r_l^{\max}\rfloor)$
\FOR{each layer $l$}
  \STATE $r_l\leftarrow r_{\min}
    +\bigl\lfloor\frac{\cH_l^{1/\tau}}{\sum_j\cH_j^{1/\tau}}
    (\cB-L\cdot r_{\min})\bigr\rceil$
    \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:rank-alloc}}
  \STATE Clip: $r_l\leftarrow\min(r_l,\,r_l^{\max})$
\ENDFOR
\STATE Greedy correction to ensure $\sum_l r_l=\cB$.
\medskip
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 3: Decomposition \& Module Construction}
\FOR{each layer $l$}
  \STATE Retrieve cached
    $\widetilde{\bU}_r,\widetilde{\bSigma}_r,\widetilde{\bV}_r,\bC$
    (truncated to rank $r_l$)
  \STATE $\bW^{\down}\leftarrow\widetilde{\bSigma}_r^{1/2}
    \widetilde{\bV}_r^\top(\bC^{(l)})^{-1}$
    \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:init-down}}
  \FOR{$h=1,\dots,H$}
    \STATE $\bW_h^{\up}\leftarrow
      \widetilde{\bU}_r\widetilde{\bSigma}_r^{1/2}
      +\epsilon_h$,\;
      $\sum_h\epsilon_h=\bm{0}$
      \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:init-up}}
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Construct \texttt{DecoupledGatedSVDConv2d} with gate input
    chosen by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate-decouple}
\ENDFOR
\medskip
\STATE \textbf{// Phase 4: End-to-End Training}
\FOR{each epoch}
  \FOR{each mini-batch $(\bX,y)$}
    \STATE Forward pass; compute $\cL_{\mathrm{task}}$
      (with optional KD, Eq.~\eqref{eq:kd})
    \STATE Compute $\cL_{\mathrm{bal}}$
      from cached gating probabilities
      \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:balance}}
    \STATE $\cL\leftarrow\cL_{\mathrm{task}}
      +\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}\,\bar{\cL}_{\mathrm{bal}}$
      \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:total-loss}}
    \STATE Backward; update $\theta$.
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We now show that the modifications introduced by HeteroInherNet-IB are
not only practically motivated but also \emph{theoretically grounded}.
Our analysis proceeds along three axes: tighter approximation bounds
(Section~\ref{sec:theory-approx}), preservation of convergence guarantees
(Section~\ref{sec:theory-conv}), and information-theoretic consistency
(Section~\ref{sec:theory-ib}).

% ---------- 5.1 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Improved Approximation via Data-Weighted Truncation}
\label{sec:theory-approx}

\begin{theorem}[Data-weighted Eckart--Young--Mirsky]
\label{thm:weighted-eym}
Let $\bW\in\R^{m\times n}$ be a weight matrix with input covariance
$\bSigma_x\succ 0$ and Cholesky factor $\bC$.
Among all rank-$r$ matrices $\hat{\bW}$, the truncated SVD of the
whitened weight $\widetilde{\bW}=\bW\bC$ yields the minimum
data-weighted approximation error:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\bW}^*
  = \arg\min_{\rank(\hat{\bW})\le r}
    \|\bW-\hat{\bW}\|_{\bSigma_x}^2
  \triangleq
    \arg\min_{\rank(\hat{\bW})\le r}
    \tr\!\bigl[(\bW-\hat{\bW})\bSigma_x(\bW-\hat{\bW})^\top\bigr].
\end{equation}
Moreover, the minimum error equals
$\sum_{i=r+1}^{\min(m,n)}\tilde{\sigma}_i^2$,
where $\tilde{\sigma}_i$ are the singular values of
$\widetilde{\bW}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any candidate $\hat{\bW}$, define
$\hat{\bA}=\hat{\bW}\bC$ and $\bA=\bW\bC$.  Since $\bC$ is invertible,
$\rank(\hat{\bA})=\rank(\hat{\bW})$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \|\bW-\hat{\bW}\|_{\bSigma_x}^2
  =
  \|(\bW-\hat{\bW})\bC\|_F^2
  =
  \|\bA-\hat{\bA}\|_F^2 .
\end{equation}
Thus the data-weighted problem is exactly the standard rank-$r$
Frobenius approximation problem for $\bA=\widetilde{\bW}$.  By
Eckart--Young--Mirsky, the optimizer is
$\hat{\bA}^*=\widetilde{\bU}_r\widetilde{\bSigma}_r
\widetilde{\bV}_r^\top$, and therefore
$\hat{\bW}^*=\hat{\bA}^*\bC^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:weighted-preservation}
Under HeteroInherNet-IB's whitened decomposition, the knowledge
preservation rate (cf.\ Proposition~2.7 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet})
is replaced by the sharper data-aligned bound:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Sim}_{\bSigma_x}(f_{\bW},f_r)
  \ge 1 - \sum_{l=1}^{L}\alpha_l
    \biggl(1-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r_l}\tilde{\sigma}_{l,i}^2}
                   {\sum_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{l,j}^2}\biggr),
\end{equation}
where similarity is now measured under the data distribution rather
than uniformly.
\end{corollary}

This result directly motivates the heterogeneous rank allocation:
layers with slowly decaying whitened spectra (high $\cH_l$) need
larger $r_l$ to achieve the same similarity threshold, while layers
with rapidly decaying spectra can be compressed more aggressively.

% ---------- 5.2 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Preservation of Convergence Guarantees}
\label{sec:theory-conv}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence compatibility under SGD]
\label{thm:conv-hetero}
Under Assumptions~2.1--2.3 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet} (Lipschitz
gradient, bounded variance, bounded representations), and assuming
bounded routing features, bounded expert perturbations, and bounded
load-balance gradients, the SGD iterates
$\{\theta^{(t)}\}_{t=1}^T$ of HeteroInherNet-IB satisfy the same
stationarity-rate order as the original InherNet analysis, with
modified constants:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \E\!\bigl[\|\nabla\cL(\theta^{(t)})\|^2\bigr]
  \le
  \mathrm{Rate}_{\mathrm{InherNet}}(T)
  + \cO(\alpha^2+\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The proof follows the same structure as Theorem~B.3
of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}.  The critical observation is that
HeteroInherNet-IB's modifications (heterogeneous ranks, whitened
initialization, decoupled gating, noise perturbation) affect only
the \emph{constants} in the smoothness and variance bounds, not
the functional form of the SGD descent lemma.  Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
\item Per-layer rank heterogeneity changes the effective
  parameter count and the Lipschitz constant $L$ layer-by-layer,
  but global smoothness still holds under a finite maximum layer
  constant.
\item The whitened SVD initialization remains an orthogonal
  initialization in the data-weighted coordinates, so the same
  conditioning intuition applies after absorbing the covariance
  conditioning into the constants.
\item The noise perturbation $\epsilon_h$ has zero mean and
  bounded variance ($\alpha^2\|\cdot\|^2$), which adds only
  $\cO(\alpha^2)$ to the variance bound.
\item The load-balance loss has bounded gradient
  $\|\nabla\cL_{\mathrm{bal}}\|\le H$ (since $\bar{G}_h\le 1$
  and the softmax Jacobian is bounded), contributing
  $\cO(\lambda_{\mathrm{bal}}H)$ to the total gradient norm.
\end{itemize}
Substituting these modified constants into the descent argument used
for InherNet yields the stated compatibility result.  This theorem
should be presented as an assumption-conditional extension, not as a
claim that HeteroInherNet universally improves the asymptotic rate.
\end{proof}

% ---------- 5.3 -----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Information-Bottleneck Consistency}
\label{sec:theory-ib}

Appendix~A.2 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet} establishes that the
one-down-many-ups design creates a single shared bottleneck forming the
Markov chain $\bX\to\bZ\to\bY$, and the IB Lagrangian is
$\cL_{\mathrm{IB}}=I(\bX;\bZ)-\beta\,I(\bZ;\bY)$.

HeteroInherNet-IB preserves this topology: the shared projection
$\bW^{\down}$ still produces a single latent variable
$\bZ=\bW^{\down}(\bX)\in\R^{r_l}$, and the expert heads
$\{\bW_h^{\up}\}$ operate on this shared representation.  The
modifications are consistent with the IB objective under a
linear-Gaussian or local-linear approximation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Whitened decomposition} aligns the SVD truncation with
  the data distribution, so discarded components have small
  data-weighted variance.  In a linear-Gaussian setting this provides
  a tractable proxy for reducing unnecessary information in
  $I(\bX;\bZ)$.
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous rank allocation} assigns higher $r_l$
  (more bottleneck capacity) to layers where $I(\bZ;\bY)$ is harder to
  preserve, and fewer bits where the spectrum decays rapidly
  (predictive information appears concentrated in a lower-dimensional
  subspace).  This follows the rate-distortion intuition behind
  IB~\cite{tishby2000information}, without requiring an exact
  nonlinear mutual-information estimate.
\item \textbf{De-symmetrized experts} enable genuine specialization
  among the heads $\bW_h^{\up}$, increasing the effective dimensionality
  of $\bY$ from $r$ (single head) to up to $rH$ (the union of expert
  subspaces), as argued in Appendix~A.2 of~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}.
\item \textbf{Load-balance loss} discourages expert collapse into a single
  active expert, maintaining the expressiveness benefit of the
  many-ups design.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Narrative Positioning for the Full Paper}
\label{sec:narrative}

The central story should be simple: InherNet shows that a compact model
can inherit a trained source by SVD-based low-rank factorization, but
uniform rank assigns the same bottleneck to every layer.  Real networks
do not distribute task-relevant information uniformly across layers.
HeteroInherNet-IB therefore turns inheritance into a data-aware budget
allocation problem.

A top-tier introduction should follow this arc:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Motivate why explicit inheritance is a useful alternative to
  designing small students or adapting frozen models.
\item Present InherNet as the right starting point because it inherits
  full trained weights rather than only matching outputs.
\item Identify the gap: uniform low-rank inheritance ignores
  layer-wise data-conditioned spectral complexity.
\item State the principle: allocate bottleneck capacity according to
  data-weighted spectral entropy under a global parameter budget.
\item Preview the evidence: a data-weighted approximation theorem, an
  entropy-budget derivation, convergence-compatible optimization
  analysis, and experiments/diagnostics showing improved
  accuracy--parameter tradeoffs.
\end{enumerate}

The paper should avoid claiming universal IB optimality.  The stronger
and more credible claim is that HeteroInherNet-IB provides a principled
data-weighted approximation and rate-distortion interpretation that
explains why heterogeneous ranks are preferable to uniform ranks.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why not jointly optimize ranks?}
One could envision a differentiable rank-selection mechanism
(e.g., Gumbel-softmax over discrete rank choices).  We deliberately
avoid this for two reasons: (i)~SVD-based decomposition is inherently
a discrete operation, and differentiable relaxations introduce
additional approximation error; (ii)~our entropy-based allocation is
performed \emph{once} before training, adding negligible cost, whereas
differentiable rank search would incur per-iteration overhead.

\paragraph{Scalability to Transformers.}
While our current implementation targets convolutional layers in
ResNet architectures, the method extends naturally to linear layers in
Transformers (e.g., attention projections and FFN layers).
The original InherNet has been demonstrated on T5 and
LLaMA-2-7B~\cite{zhou2026inherNet}; HeteroInherNet-IB's per-layer
entropy analysis is equally applicable, and the decoupled gating
generalizes to any factorized linear module.

\paragraph{Relation to LoRA and other PEFT methods.}
Unlike LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}, which decomposes the \emph{weight update}
$\Delta\bW$ with random initialization, HeteroInherNet-IB decomposes
the \emph{full pretrained weight} $\bW$ with SVD initialization.  This
enables direct knowledge inheritance rather than adaptation from a
frozen base, making it more suitable for model compression scenarios
where the goal is to produce a standalone lightweight model.
